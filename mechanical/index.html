01 — Concept

A rover that climbs by geometry

A two-stage passive bogie suspension lets all 16 wheels keep contact with uneven ground. A single central motor drives every wheel through 36-tooth pulleys (nominal 1:1) and a multi-stage TPU belt network.

Video · team kinematic study
02 — CAD

I built and owned a 115-part assembly (14 unique parts) in Fusion 360 — chassis, axles, pulleys, wheels and the belt network — as the team's lead CAD designer.

Why Outcome 1 · engineer's read of the eight
  • Supported centre hubMaterial runs right under the top bore, where the pivot load enters. C3 hangs it on one thin link that could crack; C6 adds bulk but backs it thinly.
  • Moves furtherAn open arch with nothing hanging below it: the bogie can swing further over rough ground before anything touches. C4, C5 and C8 fill that space.
  • LightestThe lightest outcome of the study (team report) — less unsprung mass on every wheel pair. C5 and C8 carry visibly more material.
  • SymmetricThe arm pivots both ways on uneven ground, so it should answer the same either way. C7 is lopsided.
